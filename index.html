Research

Language models are often confidently wrong, and the confidence they state rarely tracks how often they are right. I work on closing that gap: measuring it, and training models to report uncertainty that you can actually act on.

My current thrust is SECL. Models are better at judging an answer than at generating one, so SECL uses the model’s own P(True) as label-free supervision and distills it into its stated confidence with a small online LoRA adapter, adapting only when the input distribution shifts. Interests: uncertainty quantification, probabilistic ML, hallucination detection, trustworthy LLMs and agents, and causality in language models.
